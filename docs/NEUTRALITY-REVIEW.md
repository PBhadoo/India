# Neutrality review

Reviewer: independent session (did not draft or score the entries). Date: 2026-10-12. Scope: all 86 entries in `src/content/actions/`, the 3 items in `src/content/developing/`, and site copy in `src/pages`, `src/components`, `src/layouts`. No scores or `governmentId` values were changed. No sources were opened (no web use), so source claims were checked only against what each entry says about itself.

Method: every entry's summary, facts, stated reasons, criticism, benefits, concerns, evidence and body was read in full; wording was also screened with a pattern search for evaluative terms (regime, historic, landmark, sweeping, flagship, claims, critics, experts and similar). Scoring figures were recomputed with the repo's own `src/lib/scoring-core.ts` (centre level, as of 2026-10-12) and cross-checked against `npm run audit`.

## 2. Structural parity

## 6. Residual risks (ranked)

1. **The per-year figure is mostly an artefact of entry selection** (M1). BJP's higher per-year value is explained by 2.7 times more entries a year; its points per action are half INC's. Until entries per government-year are controlled or points per action is shown beside it, the table can be misread as a ranking, even with the caveats.
2. **Blinding is partial and the scorers are not independent.** Both scorers are AI sessions that read the facts text, which carries minister names, dates and "Janata government" style cues; the reviewer and drafter are also models. Correlated errors (for example systematic caution about recent events) are not captured by the A/B agreement figure: 22 of 86 entries have a flagged criterion and 7 have scorer totals 4 or more points apart.
3. **Recency and evidence availability** (M4). Recent governments and 1970-89 score about 3 per entry against about 10 for 1950-69 and 2004-13, partly because missing evidence scores near zero. A reader comparing eras will see a gap that is partly a method effect.
4. **Source quality is uneven and unevenly labelled.** BJP has 27% of entries with no primary source against 8% for INC; two zero-primary entries are labelled medium; 25 entries rely on unopened documents; 43 cite Wikipedia. URLs and quotations were not re-opened in this review.
5. **Criterion design** (M2, M3). Reach and Delivery supply 66% of points and Delivery can reward implementing a harmful measure. Small changes to the rubric would reorder parties in some variants.
6. **Approximate coalition shares and tenure dates** (DATA-NOTES). Shares for several governments come from general knowledge, and dates are checked against Wikipedia only. Per-year figures for coalition partners inherit that error.
7. **Selection and coverage.** 22 parties that led a government have fewer than 3 entries, 10 of 86 entries are state-level, and the choice of entries is made by maintainers. Nothing in the data tests whether the entry set is balanced by importance.
8. **The Preliminary banner is off** at 76 scored actions, although the structure suggests limited evidence for most parties other than INC and BJP.
9. **Tooling gaps:** the validator did not catch a content-format error that would have broken the build; the loaded-word list is narrow.

## 7. Recommendations

- Methodology: implement M5 (originator rule) and M6 (shrunk or pooled rates) first, because they are small and change no scores.
