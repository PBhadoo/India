## In depth

Aadhaar began as an administrative project in 2009 under the Planning Commission and operated for seven years without a dedicated statute. The 2016 Act gave it a legal basis and defined permitted uses, framed around the targeted delivery of subsidies and benefits. Because the Bill was certified as a Money Bill, it did not need the Rajya Sabha's approval, which opposition parties contested.

The Supreme Court's 2018 judgment upheld the Act by majority while narrowing it: private-sector authentication provisions were struck down, and linking was held unconstitutional for bank accounts, mobile numbers and school admissions, though it stayed mandatory for tax filing and welfare schemes. One judge dissented on both procedural and rights grounds.

Supporters cite the scale of enrolment and use, and the government has claimed large savings in welfare transfers. Critics cite reported exclusions from benefits when authentication fails, and several data-security incidents. The entry date used here is the Lok Sabha passage of the Bill (11 March 2016); the source consulted does not give the Act's commencement date, which should be confirmed from the Gazette. The savings figure is an official claim and has not been independently verified for this entry, and the Court summary cited is a secondary summary of the judgment rather than its text.
