## In depth

Before 2022 the Army, Navy and Air Force recruited personnel below officer rank on long engagements with pension entitlements, and recruitment was paused during the pandemic. Agnipath replaced this with a four-year engagement for those ranks.

The government stated the aims were a younger force and lower long-term pension costs. Pensions were around a quarter of defence spending in 2022-23. Because the first batches exit only after four years, any budgetary savings are far in the future, and the 25% retention cap means the long-term mix of the force depends on later decisions.

Critics argue that short service without pension or gratuity affects morale and the training and cohesion of units, and that most recruits will face a mid-career transition. The Delhi High Court dismissed challenges in February 2023, finding no ground to interfere with a policy decision. A Supreme Court ruling on the scheme was not found in this review.

Official intake and retention figures for later years were not retrieved; they can be found in Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha replies and should be added before scoring.
