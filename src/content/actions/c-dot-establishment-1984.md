## In depth

C-DOT was set up in August 1984 as a society under the Department of Telecommunications. Sam Pitroda returned from the United States to lead it. The organisation's stated aim was to develop digital switching technology in India within 36 months and Rs 36 crore.

Its best-known early product was the rural automatic exchange (RAX), followed by medium-size switches for towns and higher-capacity main exchanges. C-DOT transferred its designs to licensed manufacturers, who built the equipment. Wikipedia credits the rural telecom expansion with social and economic development, while noting that the sources do not give quantified before-and-after figures.

C-DOT's timeline and another C-DOT page differ on whether the first indigenous digital exchange was installed in 1986 or 1987. The claim that it caused a rise in telephone density, or the rise in public call offices, is stated in secondary accounts but was not verified with data. Confidence is therefore low, and the entry should be read as a description of institutional creation rather than a measured outcome.
