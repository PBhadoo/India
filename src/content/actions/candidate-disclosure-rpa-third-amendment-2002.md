## In depth

This entry covers a legislative response to a Supreme Court order. In May 2002 the Court asked the Election Commission to obtain affidavits from candidates on criminal cases, assets and liabilities, and education. The Centre held an all-party meeting in July 2002, and in August issued an ordinance later replaced by the Representation of the People (Third Amendment) Act.

The Act made criminal-case disclosure statutory, with a sworn affidavit and a penalty for false statements. It also provided in Section 33B that candidates need not disclose anything beyond what the Act and its rules required, regardless of court orders or Election Commission instructions.

The government stated practical concerns about verification, and parties objected to a disqualification condition. Critics argued that the Act reversed the Court's order. In March 2003 the Supreme Court struck down Section 33B and restored disclosure of assets, liabilities and education through Election Commission directions. The entry is marked struck-down because the contested part of the Act no longer operates; Section 33A and Section 125A remain.
