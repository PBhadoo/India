facts:
  - "On 2 March 2023 a five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court (Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India, W.P.(C) 104 of 2015, (2023) 6 SCC 161) held, as an interim measure until Parliament made a law under Article 324(2), that appointments of the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and Election Commissioners (ECs) are made on the recommendation of a three-member committee; the committee named in the Court's directions was the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India [3][5]."
  - "The Bill was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 10 August 2023, passed by the Rajya Sabha on 12 December 2023 and by the Lok Sabha on 21 December 2023 [2][6]. The Act (Act No. 49 of 2023) carries the date 28 December 2023 and came into force on 2 January 2024 by notification S.O. 35(E) [1]."
  - "Section 7 of the Act: the President appoints on the recommendation of a Selection Committee of the Prime Minister (Chairperson), the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha (or, if none is recognised, the leader of the single largest opposition party) and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister. An appointment is not invalid merely because of a vacancy or defect in the Committee's constitution [1]."
  - "Section 6: a Search Committee headed by the Minister of Law and Justice, with two members not below the rank of Secretary to the Government of India, prepares a panel of five names; under section 8(2) the Selection Committee may also consider persons outside that panel [1]."
  - "Section 5 limits eligibility to persons who hold or have held a post equivalent to Secretary to the Government of India and who have integrity and experience of managing and conducting elections. Section 9: a term of six years or until age 65, whichever is earlier, with no re-appointment [1]."
  - "Section 10 sets salary equal to that of a Supreme Court judge. Section 11: the CEC is removable in like manner and on like grounds as a Supreme Court judge; other ECs are removable only on the CEC's recommendation [1]. The Bill as introduced in August 2023 had instead linked pay to the Cabinet Secretary [2]; the enacted text uses the Supreme Court judge standard [1]."
  - "The Act replaced the Election Commission (Conditions of Service of Election Commissioners and Transaction of Business) Act, 1991 [2]."
  - "On 12 January 2024 the Supreme Court declined to stay the Act. On 22 March 2024 a bench of Justices Sanjiv Khanna and Dipankar Datta dismissed stay applications against section 7 in Dr. Jaya Thakur v. Union of India (2024 INSC 246), after two EC posts fell vacant and were filled on 14 March 2024 [4][6] (the 12 January refusal is as reported by SC Observer)."
  - "The writ petitions remain pending. SC Observer reports that on 23 September 2026 a bench directed the papers to the Chief Justice of India to constitute a Constitution Bench; that report was not checked against the order text [6]."

## In depth

Article 324(2) of the Constitution says the President appoints the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, subject to any law made by Parliament. No such law existed until 2023. In March 2023 the Supreme Court said that, until Parliament legislated, appointments should follow the advice of a three-member committee, and it named the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India.

Parliament passed the 2023 Act in December 2023. It provides for a Selection Committee of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister. The government's position is that this fits the Court's ruling because the Court's committee was a temporary measure and appointments are an executive function. Opposition members and several commentators say that replacing the Chief Justice with a minister gives the government side a majority on the committee. The Supreme Court has not decided that question: in March 2024 it refused to stay the law, and the challenge is pending.
