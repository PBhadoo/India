## In depth

Coal nationalisation took place in two steps. Coking coal mines, needed for steel, were taken over in 1971-72, and all remaining mines followed on 1 May 1973. Coal India Limited was formed in 1975 to run the mines as a holding company with regional subsidiaries. The Ministry of Coal gives the reasons as steel-sector needs, inadequate private investment, and concerns about mining practice and labour conditions.

Since then output has risen from tens of millions of tonnes to several hundred million tonnes at CIL alone, though demand also rose substantially over the period and the data do not isolate the effect of ownership. The public monopoly was progressively opened: allocations of captive blocks from 1993 were ruled illegal by the Supreme Court in 2014, and a 2018 Cabinet Committee decision allowed commercial mining by private firms. Captive and commercial mines now produce a growing share.

Sources differ on some dates, including the 1973 takeover (1 May versus 30 January), and on whether the 1973 Act was repealed in 2018; the repeal claim here rests on a secondary source and has not been checked against the gazette. Safety and environmental figures cited are from secondary sources and are indicative only.
