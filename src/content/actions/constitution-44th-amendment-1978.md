## In depth

The amendment was enacted by the Janata-led government elected in March 1977 after the Emergency of 1975-77. The Bill was debated in Parliament from December 1977 to December 1978; the Rajya Sabha did not adopt some clauses, and the Act took effect in stages from April to September 1979.

Its main provisions changed the conditions for declaring a national Emergency, limited the length of preventive detention without an Advisory Board, and removed the right to property from the list of fundamental rights, replacing it with a legal right. The government stated that it was guarding against fundamental rights being taken away by a transient majority.

Supporters say the amendment added procedural checks to emergency powers. Critics of particular provisions point to the loss of the fundamental right to property and to the clauses that were not enacted. This entry does not assess whether Emergency powers have been used differently since 1979, because the sources reviewed do not address that question.
