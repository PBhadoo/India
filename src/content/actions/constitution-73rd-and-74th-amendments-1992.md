## In depth

Local government is a state subject in the Constitution. The 73rd and 74th Amendments, passed in 1992 and in force in 1993, gave panchayats and urban local bodies a constitutional basis, so that states had to hold regular elections and set up certain institutions.

For panchayats, the Eleventh Schedule lists 29 subjects, such as agriculture, minor irrigation and primary education, which states may devolve. The 74th Amendment did the same for municipalities. Both reserve one-third of seats for women and require State Election Commissions and State Finance Commissions.

What the Schedule requires is a matter of interpretation. High Court rulings describe it as an enabling provision: states decide through their own laws which functions to transfer. As a result, implementation differs across states. A 2009 central government note counted 12 of 24 applicable states with District Planning Committees at that point. This entry does not report current devolution data, and the Twelfth Schedule, which lists the 18 municipal subjects, was not verified from the sources opened.
