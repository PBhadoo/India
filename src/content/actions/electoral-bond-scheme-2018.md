  - The scheme was announced by Finance Minister Arun Jaitley in the 2017-18 Budget and notified on 2 January 2018; the package also capped anonymous cash donations to parties at Rs 2,000, down from Rs 20,000 [2][3].

## In depth

The scheme replaced some cash donations with bonds bought from SBI. Purchasers were identified to the bank but not to the public, and parties encashed bonds in designated accounts. Its supporters, including the Union government in court, said it would reduce cash and protect donors from retaliation. Its challengers said it hid the source of funds from voters and could enable favours in return for donations.

On 15 February 2024 the Supreme Court held that the scheme and linked amendments to the Representation of the People Act, the Companies Act and the Income Tax Act were unconstitutional. It ordered SBI to give purchase and encashment details to the Election Commission, which published them in March 2024.

The published data showed that large sums went to both ruling and opposition parties, though in different amounts. The sources reviewed give different party totals, so this entry quotes only the figures from one report. Whether the scheme increased or decreased total unaccounted money in elections is not established by the sources reviewed.
