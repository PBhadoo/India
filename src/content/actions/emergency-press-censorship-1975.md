## In depth

Censorship during the Emergency operated through a combination of administrative guidelines, a press law enacted in 1976, control of newsprint and advertising, and the immediate disconnection of power on the night of the proclamation. The Shah Commission, appointed by the subsequent government, examined these measures and recorded its findings in interim and final reports.

The government of the time based the Emergency on internal disturbance and, in accounts of the Prime Minister's address, on an alleged conspiracy. Critics, including the Shah Commission, concluded that censorship was used for political ends. The Janata government that took office in 1977 repealed the 1976 Act.

Note that the Shah Commission was constituted by a successor government and its findings were itself contested by supporters of the earlier government; the sources reviewed here describe the report through secondary summaries rather than the report itself.
