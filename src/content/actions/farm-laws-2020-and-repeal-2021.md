- 'Three Ordinances were promulgated on 5 June 2020: the Farmers'' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation), the Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services, and the Essential Commodities (Amendment) Ordinances; Parliament passed the Acts in September 2020 [1][2].'
- The trade law allowed trade of farmers' produce outside APMC market yards at farm gates, warehouses and similar places and barred States from levying market fees there; the contract-farming law set written agreements of one season to five years with a three-level dispute process and no action against the farmer's land for recovery; the amendment limited stock limits on agricultural produce to a 100% retail price rise (horticulture) or 50% (non-perishables) [1].

- On 12 January 2021 the Supreme Court stayed implementation of the three laws, left the existing MSP system in place and formed a four-member committee (Bhupinder Singh Mann, Pramod Joshi, Ashok Gulati, Anil Ghanwat) [3].
- The committee submitted its report to the Court on 19 March 2021; it was made public in March 2022. Member Anil Ghanwat stated that 61 of 73 farmer organisations that gave views, representing 3.3 crore farmers, backed the laws; this is one member's account and has not been independently verified [4].
- The Prime Minister announced withdrawal on 19 November 2021; the Farm Laws Repeal Bill, 2021 was introduced and passed by both Houses on 29 November 2021 [2][4].

## In depth

The 2020 laws were designed to change how farmers sell and buy: produce could be sold outside APMC yards free of market fees, farmers and buyers could sign contracts before sowing, and stock limits on foods such as cereals, pulses and onions were restricted to price spikes. The Government presented this as widening choice. APMC laws were not repealed, but their reach was limited to market yards.

Farmer unions, particularly in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh, protested from late 2020, with concerns that centred on MSP-based procurement and the role of the mandis. The Supreme Court stayed the laws in January 2021 and appointed a committee, whose report was made public in March 2022. The Government announced withdrawal in November 2021 and Parliament repealed the Acts on 29 November 2021.

Because the laws were stayed within months and never fully operated, assessments rest on design and precedent rather than outcomes. PRS highlighted the Bihar experience as a caution, and also noted that most farmers lack access to APMC markets or procurement, which supported the case for alternative channels. The episode is cited by supporters of reform as an unfinished agenda and by critics as an example of reform without consultation; both views rest on interpretation rather than measured results.
