## In depth

The Farakka Barrage, commissioned in 1975, diverts Ganga water towards the Hooghly. Bangladesh, the lower riparian, objected to reduced dry-season flows, and talks produced a 1977 agreement and short memoranda in the 1980s. After these lapsed, the 1996 treaty set a 30-year framework.

The treaty does not give a single fixed share. It sets bands of flow at Farakka, with an equal split at low flows and fixed volumes at higher flows, plus a guaranteed 35,000 cusecs to each side in alternate 10-day periods in the driest part of the season. An emergency clause calls for consultation when flow is below 50,000 cusecs.

Supporters see a durable, rules-based arrangement. Critics, including analysts writing for ORF, say actual deliveries to Bangladesh in critical periods fell short in many years, that the treaty lacks an unconditional minimum guarantee, and that it addresses volume but not ecological flow. Several secondary sources differ on the exact start date of the guarantee window and on the 1977 terms, so the treaty text should be checked for exact wording. Renewal or replacement is due by December 2026.
