## In depth

The Golden Quadrilateral was the first and best-known component of the National Highways Development Project. It upgraded existing roads between the four largest metropolitan cities to four or six lanes rather than building an entirely new network, and it was executed by NHAI through contracts awarded in packages.

Evidence on outcomes is mixed in kind rather than in direction. The best-known academic evaluation, which uses enterprise-level data on organised manufacturing, finds gains in entry and productivity for districts very close to the highway. Audit and parliamentary material, by contrast, concentrate on delivery: the CAG's 2005 audit documented that the first phase was far behind its original schedule and quantified the cost of that delay to road users and to NHAI.

Reported completion dates differ by source, from a January 2012 announcement to a parliamentary document expecting full completion in December 2012. The academic findings concern one sector (organised manufacturing) and one period; they do not measure road safety, toll burden on users or environmental effects, and this entry does not draw conclusions on those.
