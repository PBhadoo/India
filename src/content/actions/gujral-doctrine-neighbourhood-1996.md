## In depth

The doctrine is a stated approach rather than a legal instrument, so its effects are hard to measure. It set out five principles for India's dealings with South Asian neighbours, with the first, non-reciprocity towards smaller neighbours, regarded as its most distinctive element.

Supporters point to the cooperation that followed with Bangladesh and Nepal in 1996, including water treaties. Critics note that the doctrine did not cover Pakistan, that four of the five principles restate common norms, and that later commentary linked it to security debates. The attribution of intelligence consequences is made by commentators and is not established in the sources reviewed here.

This entry's evidence is mostly secondary and think-tank commentary. The Chatham House speech text was not retrieved, so the exact date and wording of the principles should be checked against the original before the entry is treated as settled.
