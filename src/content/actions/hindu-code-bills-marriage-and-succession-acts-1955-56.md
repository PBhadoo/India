## In depth

The Hindu Code was first drafted by a committee associated with B. R. Ambedkar. After a long dispute, the government passed its provisions as separate Acts between 1955 and 1956, covering marriage, succession, minority and guardianship, and adoption and maintenance. This entry covers the Marriage and Succession Acts, the two with the widest reach.

Supporters of the reform pointed to the need to set common statutory rules, including on divorce and women's property. Opponents argued that Hindu marriage is a sacrament and that the changes would affect the joint family. The Succession Act kept the coparcenary (joint family property) system, under which daughters were not equal to sons until Parliament amended it in 2005. The Supreme Court's 2020 decision then settled how the 2005 amendment applies to daughters born before it.

The Acts did not extend to Muslims, Christians or Parsis, and Article 44 of the Constitution, which mentions a uniform civil code, is not enforceable by courts. Figures on marriages, divorces or inheritance outcomes are not summarised here and were not verified for this entry.
