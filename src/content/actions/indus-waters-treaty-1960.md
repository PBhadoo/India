## In depth

The treaty divides the Indus system by river rather than by volume. The three eastern rivers went to India for unrestricted use and the three western rivers went to Pakistan, with India allowed specific non-consumptive and limited uses on the western rivers. Annexures set design limits for run-of-river hydropower, which is the source of most later disputes.

Supporters point to its long operation, its technical dispute routes and the fact that the 1960 text set out rights and obligations in detail. Critics in India argue that the allocation by volume favours Pakistan and that India's permitted uses on the western rivers remain under-developed. Pakistan's position is that the lower riparian needs the treaty's certainty.

India put the treaty in abeyance on 23 April 2025. The treaty text itself has no provision for unilateral suspension, and the Court of Arbitration, which India does not recognise, has issued awards on matters including the status of the treaty. The legal position is therefore disputed between the two countries. Figures for flows and storage differ slightly between sources and should be checked against the treaty annexures.
