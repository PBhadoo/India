## In depth

The July 1991 industrial policy statement was one part of a larger package that included exchange-rate, trade and fiscal changes under an IMF-supported programme. The industrial changes removed most licensing, narrowed public-sector reservation and allowed higher foreign equity through automatic approval.

Supporters link the package to the recovery of reserves and to faster growth through the 1990s. Critics note that the reforms were partial and uneven, that growth did not generate commensurate formal employment, and that debate continues over how much the IMF programme shaped the content. Both views draw on the same period of data, and attribution is difficult because several policy changes, and global conditions overlapped.

This entry deals with the industrial policy statement and the broader reform package as a whole; the individual trade and exchange-rate measures were not separately verified here.
