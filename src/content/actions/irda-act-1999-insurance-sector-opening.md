## In depth

Life insurance was nationalised in 1956 under the Life Insurance Corporation, and general insurance companies were later brought under public-sector ownership. In 1994 the Malhotra Committee recommended a regulator and wider participation. The IRDA Act of December 1999 created the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority, which began functioning in April 2000.

The regulator invited applications for registration in August 2000. Foreign companies could hold up to 26% of an insurer's equity at first. The cap was later changed: Wikipedia reports a rise to 74% in the 2021 Union Budget, and another summary mentions a 49% rule in 2015, which is not mentioned on the Wikipedia page, so the interim step is unverified.

The sources reviewed give the legal framework and the count of insurers but not outcome measures such as insurance penetration or the LIC's market share over time. Section references and amendment details were not verified from the Act's text. For that reason confidence is low, and outcome scores should be treated as provisional.
