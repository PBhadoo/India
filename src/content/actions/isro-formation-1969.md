## In depth

India's space programme began with INCOSPAR in 1962, under the Department of Atomic Energy and led by Vikram Sarabhai. ISRO took its name in 1969, and in 1972 the government created a Space Commission and a Department of Space, with ISRO placed under it.

The institutional choice was to run space activity as a government department with a long-term mandate. Supporters point to the continuity this gave and to the capabilities that later supported technology transfer to industry. Critics point to the dependence on annual budgets and to the limited role of private firms until the reforms of 2020.

This entry records the creation of the institution rather than individual missions such as satellite launches or lunar and Mars missions, which would be separate entries with their own evidence. Sources differ on exact dates for 1969 and 1972, and the outcome figures for the early decades (launch counts, costs) were not retrieved, so confidence is low.
