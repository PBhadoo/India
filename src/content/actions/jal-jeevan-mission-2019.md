## In depth

The Jal Jeevan Mission shifted the focus of rural drinking water policy from habitations served by public sources to a functional tap connection in each household, at a minimum service level of 55 litres per capita per day. Delivery is by states, with central funding, community committees (Pani Samitis) and third-party inspection before payment.

On the Government's data, coverage rose from 17% to nearly 80% of rural households within about five and a half years, though the original 2024 target for full coverage was missed and the mission has been extended, according to reports. Because coverage is reported through a state-fed dashboard, other evidence matters: a November 2025 account of central officers' ground checks reported that around a quarter of villages under large schemes had non-functional supply, and the Centre penalised seven states for irregularities.

Health and time-saving benefits cited by the Government are projections by WHO and others, not measured results of the mission. This entry is marked as developing because the programme is ongoing, its deadline and funding were being revised in 2026, and the audit figures come from a news report that did not name an audit body. Outlay figures are omitted because sources conflict.
