## In depth

The National Solar Mission was one of the missions under the National Action Plan on Climate Change adopted in 2008, and was launched in January 2010. Its first phase used a bundling model, in which solar power from developers chosen by reverse auction was sold together with cheaper power from central generation, and its second phase relied on viability gap funding. A later Budget raised the long-term target from 20 GW to 100 GW.

Installed solar capacity grew from a few hundred megawatts at most in 2010 to more than 100 GW by 2025 and about 171 GW in Ministry data for September 2026. Auction tariffs fell sharply over the decade. Contributors to that outcome include global module price declines, state policies and competitive auctions as well as the Mission itself, and this entry does not separate them.

Concerns include grid integration, curtailment and the concentration of capacity in a few states. The domestic content requirement was intended to support local manufacturing, drew a WTO complaint from the United States, and led some developers to use imported thin-film modules; the outcome of the WTO case was not verified for this entry. The 2010 baseline differs between the two Wikipedia pages used, and the Ministry's page carries inconsistent date labels, so the latest figure should be checked against the Ministry's current release.
