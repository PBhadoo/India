## In depth

The campaign reorganised how Kerala spent a large share of its plan funds. Instead of departments deciding every scheme, 35 to 40 percent of the plan outlay went to elected local bodies, which prepared projects after village assemblies identified problems. The process then moved upward through block and district councils.

The main evidence opened for this entry is a health-sector evaluation by authors who describe the campaign positively but also list shortcomings: limited local drafting experience and parts of the health system that stayed centralised. One of the authors co-wrote a book with the campaign's organiser, so a reader may want a separate, independent evaluation.

Kerala later saw a change of government in 2001; this entry does not assess later developments in local government finance. No primary government document (plan orders, Planning Board reports) was opened, so participation numbers and the campaign's start date remain unverified and the confidence rating is low.
