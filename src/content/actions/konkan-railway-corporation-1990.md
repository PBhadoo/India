## In depth

The Konkan line was proposed in several earlier decades and surveyed in the 1970s, but construction began only after 1989-90, when the Centre agreed to a separate company rather than building it as an ordinary Railway Board project. The company's account is that funding from the Centre and four states, and a lean field organisation of seven sectors, allowed construction within a fixed time limit. The terrain was difficult: monsoon rains, landslides and soft soil caused repeated tunnel collapses, and the project recorded 74 deaths during construction.

The line opened in stages, with full services from January 1998. A case study of the company's early years describes revenue below projections and substantial debt, and the company first reported a profit in FY 2009-10. Supporters point to the line's role in west-coast connectivity and to the RORO truck-carrying service; critics have pointed to environmental objections raised in Goa, to accidents after opening, and to the financial burden of the early years.

Most figures here come from the company's own history and from secondary summaries, and construction cost and debt figures come from a single case study. Readers should treat the financial figures as indicative rather than audited.
