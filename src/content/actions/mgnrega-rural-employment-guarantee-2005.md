## In depth

The Act turned rural public works into a legal entitlement: any rural household whose adult members asked for unskilled work was to be offered it, up to a limit per household per year, with wages paid directly. The scheme became one of the largest public works programmes in the world by households covered, and demand rose sharply in 2020-21.

Evaluations vary in what they measure. One randomised study found that an improvement in payment delivery raised incomes and rural wages in Andhra Pradesh. Standing Committee reports record shortfalls between demand and allocation, delays in wages and compensation, and uneven social audits. The proportion of households reaching the 100-day limit was much lower than the number employed.

In December 2025 Parliament replaced the Act with a new law that raises the limit to 125 days, introduces State-wise normative allocations and a seasonal pause window, and changes the cost-sharing ratio. The Union government presented these as corrections to design weaknesses; critics argued that a demand-driven right had become a supply-driven scheme and that States would carry more cost. The new Act took effect on 1 July 2026, so evidence on its operation is not yet available.
