## In depth

NEP 2020 is a policy document, not a statute, so its provisions reach practice through central schemes, regulators, curricula and state decisions. Education is on the Concurrent List, which means many measures need state agreement.

The policy restructures school years, proposes board-exam reform and four-year undergraduate programmes, and sets enrolment and spending targets. Its language text says that teaching up to at least Grade 5 should be in the mother tongue or regional language and that no language will be imposed. States differ on how they read and apply this.

Supporters point to the early-childhood focus, flexible degrees and rising higher-education enrolment. Critics, including the Tamil Nadu government, raise concerns about centralisation, language and funding conditions. Official data show enrolment rising, but the trend began before 2020 and the policy's longer-run outcomes cannot yet be measured. A bill to create a single higher-education regulator was introduced in December 2025 and, in the latest reports reviewed, was still before a Joint Parliamentary Committee.
