## In depth

The NIA Act created a central agency whose officers have police powers across India for offences listed in a schedule, which includes offences under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act and the Atomic Energy Act. The schedule was widened in 2019. The Centre can direct the agency to take over a case and can constitute Special Courts.

Reported official figures show a high conviction rate among decided cases. A critical investigation by The Wire argues that a significant share of convictions involved guilty pleas after long pre-trial detention. The two sets of figures use different bases: the official figure is per decided case, while the investigation analyses the manner in which cases were concluded. Neither set of figures addresses whether the agency has reduced attacks.

The Act altered the balance between the Centre and States in the investigation of terror cases. Supporters say the nature of the offences needs national coordination; critics raised concerns about federalism at the time of passage. No successful constitutional challenge to the Act was found in the sources reviewed.
