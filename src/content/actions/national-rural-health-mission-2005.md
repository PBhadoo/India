## In depth

The Mission was a framework for central funding of rural health systems through State health societies, with additional money for facilities, staff and untied funds. Two of its most visible parts were the ASHA, a village-level health worker, and the Janani Suraksha Yojana, which paid a cash incentive to women giving birth in a health facility. The Mission was later combined with an urban component as the National Health Mission.

The clearest change in survey data is in institutional births, which rose from 39% in 2005-06 to 89% in 2019-21. The first Common Review Mission and later reviews associate the early increase with JSY and ASHAs, though the surveys cannot isolate the Mission from other influences such as income growth and State programmes. Maternal and infant mortality fell over the period, but at the 2017 CAG audit they remained above the targets set for 2015.

Audits and review missions describe gaps in facilities, specialist doctors and drug availability, and slow use of funds. The Common Review Mission noted that rising demand for institutional delivery outpaced growth in infrastructure and personnel. The Economic Survey 2025-26 credits the National Health Mission and Ayushman Bharat jointly for improvements in infant and maternal mortality, which means the data do not allow outcomes to be assigned to this Mission alone.
