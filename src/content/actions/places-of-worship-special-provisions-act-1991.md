  - "The Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha by Home Minister S.B. Chavan on 23 August 1991. Reports give 10 September 1991 for Lok Sabha passage, 12 September for the Rajya Sabha and 18 September 1991 for assent [3]."
  - "Section 3 bars conversion of a place of worship of one religious denomination or sect into one of a different denomination or sect. Section 4(1) declares that the religious character of a place of worship shall continue as it existed on 15 August 1947 [1]."
  - "Section 4(2) abates pending suits and proceedings on such conversion and bars fresh ones, with a proviso for cases where the change occurred after 15 August 1947. ## In depth

The Act applies a cut-off date of 15 August 1947 to every place of worship other than the Ayodhya site and ancient monuments covered by archaeological law. Supporters say the date draws a line under historical disputes and protects communal harmony. Challengers say it forecloses legal remedies, uses an arbitrary date, and treats one site differently.

On the legal question, the Supreme Court has not issued a final ruling on the Act's validity in the sources reviewed. In M. Siddiq (2019) the Court described the Act in terms of secularism, but whether those observations bind a later challenge is disputed: one reading treats them as an answer to the present petitions, another as obiter. In December 2024 the Court interim-restricted new suits and effective orders in pending suits while it considers the batch.
