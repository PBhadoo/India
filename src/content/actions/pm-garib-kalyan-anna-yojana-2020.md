## In depth

PMGKAY began in April 2020 as a relief measure during the COVID-19 lockdown. It gave NFSA beneficiaries an extra 5 kg of grain per person per month free of cost, on top of their normal entitlement at subsidised prices. In November 2023 the Cabinet decided to provide free grain to the same beneficiaries for five years from January 2024.

Official sources give the scale: more than 80 crore beneficiaries and a first-phase cost above Rs 75,000 crore, followed by an estimated Rs 11.80 lakh crore over five years. The Government presents it as a food and nutrition security measure for the poor. The Economic Survey describes it as part of food management supported by technology-based targeting.

The sources reviewed do not include an independent evaluation of nutrition or poverty outcomes from the scheme. The questions raised in the material are about scale and design: the fiscal commitment, an entitlement fixed to 2011 population data, and the role of database checks in identifying ineligible entries. Court hearings on migrant labourers have also considered access to ration cards.

Because the scheme uses the NFSA structure, its effects are difficult to separate from the Act (see the National Food Security Act entry), and it may be read as a change in price of the same entitlement rather than as a separate programme.
