facts:
  - "The Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 received assent on 17 January 2003 and came into force on 1 July 2005, with enforcement assigned to the Enforcement Directorate (ED) [3]."
  - "The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 (Act 23 of 2019) amended the PMLA. A Lok Sabha reply states it inserted a proviso to s.44(1)(b) from 1 August 2019 requiring closure reports in cases where no offence is made out [4]. A note filed in the Supreme Court lists the Act as amending ss.3, 44(1)(b), 44(1)(d) and 45(2), among others [5]. That note is a submission and not a court finding."
  - "Section 45 (twin bail conditions) was set aside by the Supreme Court on 24 November 2017 in Nikesh Tarachand Shah and re-enacted in revised form by Act 13 of 2018 [1][3]."
  - "On 27 July 2022 a three-judge bench (Khanwilkar, Maheshwari and Ravikumar JJ.) in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India upheld ss.5, 19, 24, 44 and 50 and the 2018 form of s.45; held that the ECIR is an internal document that need not be supplied to the accused if the grounds of arrest are disclosed; held that the 2019 Explanation to s.3 is clarificatory; and left to the larger bench in Rojer Mathew whether some amendments could be made through a Finance Act [1]."
  - "On 22 to 25 August 2022 the Court accepted a review petition and said two aspects needed reconsideration: non-supply of the ECIR and the reversal of the burden of proof [3]. On 31 July 2025 a bench headed by Justice Surya Kant began hearing the review petitions, starting with maintainability; nothing was decided on the merits [2]."
  - "On 3 October 2023 Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India required written grounds of arrest to be furnished to the arrested person [6]. On 9 August 2024 the Court granted bail to Manish Sisodia, saying that where delay is coupled with long incarceration the right to bail has to be read into s.45 [7]."
  - "Parliamentary replies show 5,422 PMLA cases and 23 convictions to 31 March 2022, and 6,312 cases and 120 convictions over about 11 years 7 months to late 2025; the two periods and measures differ [4][8]."

## In depth

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act was enacted in 2003 under the Vajpayee-era government and has been amended repeatedly, including by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 under the Modi-era government. The 2019 changes touched the definition of the offence, the ED's closure of cases and the court process; a note filed in the Supreme Court lists the sections, but the text of the Act was not opened for this entry [5].

In July 2022 the Supreme Court upheld the ED's central powers and the stringent bail conditions in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary, while saying the ED must disclose grounds of arrest [1]. Review petitions on at least two aspects are pending [2]. Later rulings added limits, including written grounds of arrest [6] and bail where there is long delay [7].

Official figures show more registered cases and searches after 2019 [4], but only a small number of convictions relative to cases registered [4][8]. The ED cites a high rate in decided cases [9]. These are different measures, and the date of the 2019 Act's assent (1 August 2019, the date the closure-report proviso took effect) and the exact text of each 2019 change should be confirmed from the Gazette.
