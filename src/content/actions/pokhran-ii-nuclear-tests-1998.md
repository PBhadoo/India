## In depth

The tests of 11 and 13 May 1998 were the first by India since 1974. On 27 May the Prime Minister told Parliament that India was observing a voluntary moratorium on further underground tests, and that it would follow a no-first-use policy and use the weapons only for self-defence.

International reaction included condemnation and sanctions under US law. Pakistan tested on 28 May 1998. Reports indicate the US lifted most sanctions within about six months and waived the remainder in September 2001, and the finance minister of the day said sanctions had limited effect beyond some defence supplies.

Supporters view the tests as establishing a credible deterrent and a declared doctrine. Critics view them as having triggered a regional arms race and as damaging non-proliferation norms. The yield and the reasons for the tests were described in the government's paper tabled in Parliament, which was not retrieved in full; the entry relies on search summaries of the statement and on secondary reporting for the sanctions record.
