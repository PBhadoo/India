## In depth

The Act converts Article 21A into a statutory entitlement. It sets norms for schools, such as pupil-teacher ratios, and bars capitation fees and admission screening. It also creates School Management Committees. Its most debated provision is Section 12(1)(c), which asks non-minority private unaided schools to reserve 25% of entry-level seats for children from disadvantaged groups, with the State reimbursing the school.

The Supreme Court upheld the Act in 2012 but excluded minority institutions. Survey data show high enrolment, but enrolment was already high before 2010, and learning-level surveys such as ASER show that around half of Std V children in rural India could read a Std II text in 2018.

Supporters point to the legal right, the school norms and the private-school admissions route. Critics, including some educationists, argue that the Act focuses on inputs and on inclusion in private schools rather than on common quality standards. Researchers who studied the quota found that implementation varied sharply between states. Evidence on how far the Act itself changed enrolment or learning is limited, because several trends began earlier.
