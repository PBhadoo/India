## In depth

The Act gives any citizen a right to request information held by a public authority, sets response timelines for designated officers, and provides a first appeal within the authority and a second appeal or complaint to an information commission. Supporters, including civil society groups that use the law, point to its reach and to court rulings widening its scope. Critics and monitoring groups focus on how the commissions function: vacancies, pendency, the share of cases returned without a hearing, and limited use of penalties.

The 2019 amendment is a separate point of dispute. The government's position, as reported in commentary, was that equating commissioners with Election Commissioners was anomalous for a statutory body; petitioners argued that executive control over tenure and pay affects independence. The Supreme Court issued notice on the challenge; its final disposal has not been verified for this entry.

The caseload figures above are compiled by a civil society group from RTI replies and are not the commissions' own consolidated statistics; the CIC's own annual report figures for 2023-24 could not be retrieved.
