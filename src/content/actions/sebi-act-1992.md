## In depth

Until the late 1980s, the Controller of Capital Issues approved company share issues under a 1947 Act. SEBI was set up as an administrative body in April 1988. In 1992 Parliament passed the SEBI Act, which gave it statutory powers. The Act is deemed to have come into force on 30 January 1992, which reflects the earlier ordinance, and it received assent on 4 April 1992.

SEBI's board includes a chairman nominated by the Union Government and members from the Finance Ministry and the Reserve Bank of India. It makes regulations, investigates and adjudicates. Orders can be appealed to the Securities Appellate Tribunal, and then to the Supreme Court.

Evaluations differ. Supporters point to reforms in settlement and depositories that came after the regulator was empowered. Critics point to later governance failures in individual cases and to allegations about the regulator's independence. The sources reviewed do not quantify market outcomes, and the shock tag refers to a market scandal of 1992 that this entry did not separately verify, so confidence is low.
