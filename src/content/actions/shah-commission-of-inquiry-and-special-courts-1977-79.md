## In depth

After the 1977 election, the Janata government appointed a commission under the Commissions of Inquiry Act, 1952 to examine excesses committed during the Emergency. Justice J. C. Shah conducted public hearings, took testimony and filed reports in 1978.

The Commission's findings addressed press censorship, detentions under MISA, the Turkman Gate incident and sterilisation drives. It concluded that the decision to declare the Emergency was not justified by the circumstances. Indira Gandhi disputed its legitimacy and declined to give evidence on oath.

To try those named, the government introduced a Special Courts Bill, on which the Supreme Court gave an advisory opinion in December 1978. The Act was passed in 1979, but the Janata government fell on 16 July 1979, and after the return of Indira Gandhi's government the special courts were found not to be legally constituted. As a result, the findings were not tested in a criminal trial. Readers should note that most of the detail here comes from a secondary account, and the Court's full reasoning on Articles 14 and 21 was not reviewed.
