## In depth

Until 2020, private companies in India largely supplied components and subsystems to ISRO. The June 2020 decisions created IN-SPACe as an authorising and promotional body and used NSIL as the commercial arm. The stated purpose was to let ISRO concentrate on research and exploration while private firms took part in launches, satellites and services.

By August 2026 the government reported 113 authorisations to 52 non-government entities and about 440 registered space startups. Private launches were still listed as proposed for 2026-27, so most of the measurable outcomes concern the number of participants rather than revenue or launches.

Supporters point to the growth in participants and the formal process. Open questions in the data are how much of the activity depends on ISRO facilities and on government demand, and when private launches will occur. This review did not find an independent evaluation, and counts differ between sources, so confidence is low. The legislative framework (a space activities law) was not covered in the sources retrieved.
