## In depth

Tamil Nadu raised its reservation in stages between 1971 and 1990, under both DMK and AIADMK governments. The Supreme Court's 1992 judgment in Indra Sawhney held that reservations should generally not exceed 50% of posts or seats. With 69% already in force, the state faced a ruling that its allocation was above the limit.

The state's response, passed in 1993 and enacted as Act 45 of 1994, was a law supported by an all-party Assembly resolution, followed by placement in the Ninth Schedule, which shields laws from certain challenges under Article 31B. The origin of the 69% total (Karunanidhi, 1989-90) and its legislative protection (Jayalalithaa, 1993-94) belong to different governments, which is why two government ids appear in the front-matter.

The state's stated purpose in 1993 was the welfare and advancement of backward classes. Challengers say the Ninth Schedule protection is unconstitutional. The exact assent date and the present status of pending cases were not verified.
