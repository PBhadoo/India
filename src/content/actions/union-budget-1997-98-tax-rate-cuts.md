## In depth

The 1997-98 Budget reduced headline direct tax rates, simplified parts of the customs structure and offered a time-limited disclosure scheme. The government stated that lower rates would improve compliance. Revenue figures at the time came in below projections for indirect taxes, while the disclosure scheme exceeded expectations according to press reports.

Interpretation differs. Supporters argue that the lower rates contributed to the later rise in tax-to-GDP ratios; contemporaries and later commentators note that the same year saw a pay-commission award, a change of government and the East Asian financial crisis, all of which affected the fiscal outcome. The sources used here are press and commentary pieces; the Budget speech and Budget documents could not be opened for this entry, so figures should be confirmed against them. Rates for corporate tax differ between sources because of the surcharge treatment.
