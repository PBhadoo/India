## In depth

The 1997-98 Budget reduced headline direct tax rates, simplified parts of the customs structure and offered a time-limited disclosure scheme. The government stated that lower rates would improve compliance. Revenue figures at the time came in below projections for indirect taxes, while the disclosure scheme exceeded expectations according to press reports.

Interpretation differs. Supporters argue that the lower rates contributed to the later rise in tax-to-GDP ratios; contemporaries and later commentators note that the same year saw a pay-commission award, a change of government and the East Asian financial crisis, all of which affected the fiscal outcome. The Budget speech was opened and confirms the personal, corporate (35% domestic, 48% foreign), dividend, customs and disclosure-scheme proposals; the remaining outcome figures (revenue shortfalls, deficit, VDIS receipts) rest on press and commentary pieces, and the Finance Act and CBDT circulars could not be opened. The speech gives the VDIS State share as 77.5 per cent, while Outlook reports about 70 per cent; the speech governs for the proposal. Corporate rates in secondary sources differ because of surcharge treatment.
