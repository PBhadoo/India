## In depth

The Act set a framework rather than a fixed benefit. A national board was to recommend schemes, and State governments were to register workers and run welfare boards. It did not create an entitlement with its own funding line, so outcomes depended on the schemes later notified and on State action.

The Lok Sabha reply states that scheme-wise beneficiary counts are not maintained centrally, which limits any assessment of coverage. The registration drive that followed under e-Shram reached far more workers than earlier efforts, but the commentary cited notes it provides limited benefits and no separate legal basis. The Act is reported to have been replaced by the Code on Social Security, 2020, now in force as part of the 2025 Labour Codes.

The baseline sentence, the replacement date and the exact scheme list of the Act were not verified from primary sources. The two parliamentary replies were found through search and their pages could not be opened; their content is as summarised in search results.
