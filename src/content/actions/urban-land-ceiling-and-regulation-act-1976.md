## In depth

The Act applied to vacant land in larger urban agglomerations. Holders above the ceiling were to declare the excess, which States could acquire at fixed low rates, with exemptions possible for specific uses. The stated objective was an equitable distribution of urban land and a check on speculation.

Government statements in 1999 reported that little of the estimated excess had come into State possession and that the Act had raised land prices and slowed housing. A 2006 working paper from a think tank, written as an argument for repeal, reached similar conclusions, while noting that some acquired land was used for housing. Defenders of the objective argue that the problem was implementation and low compensation rather than the principle of limiting concentration. Independent studies of the price effects were not found in the sources opened here.

The repeal followed Article 252: Parliament passed a law applicable first to Haryana, Punjab and the Union Territories, and States adopted it at different dates. Maharashtra's repeal in 2007 was reported as a condition for a JNNURM grant.
