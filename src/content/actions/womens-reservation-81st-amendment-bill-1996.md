## In depth

The 81st Amendment Bill of 1996 was the first of several Constitution amendment bills proposing to reserve one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies for women. It was introduced on 12 September 1996 and was referred to a Joint Committee of Parliament, chaired by Geeta Mukherjee, which reported on 9 December 1996.

The committee examined alternatives such as party quotas for nominations and dual-member constituencies and rejected both. Two Samajwadi Party members dissented on the drafting. Later bills in 1998 and 1999 lapsed with the dissolution of their Lok Sabhas, and the 2008 bill, which carried over five of the committee's seven recommendations, passed the Rajya Sabha in 2010 but lapsed in 2014.

In 2023 Parliament enacted the 106th Amendment, which reserves about one-third of seats but becomes operative only after delimitation following a census. Because this entry records the 1996 introduction, which did not itself become law, its status is shown as stalled; the later enactment is described only as context. The share of women in the Lok Sabha over the period was not verified here.
