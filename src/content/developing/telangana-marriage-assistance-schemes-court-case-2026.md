title: Telangana High Court case on the Kalyana Lakshmi and Shaadi Mubarak schemes
date: 2026-09-02
category: social-welfare
summary: A Telangana High Court division bench on 1-2 September 2026 stayed a single judge's interim order that had paused the state's marriage-assistance schemes; the petition on their legal basis remains pending.
status: developing
knownFacts:
  - "Kalyana Lakshmi and Shaadi Mubarak were introduced in 2014 through Government Orders and give financial assistance for the marriage of unmarried women from Scheduled Caste, Scheduled Tribe and minority communities; they were later extended to Backward Class and Economically Backward Class categories [1]."
  - "Advocate Vijay Gopal, appearing in person, challenged the legal and legislative basis of eight of the Government Orders; his argument as reported is that the schemes run on Government Orders not backed by state legislation [1][2]."
  - "On 12 August 2026 a single judge of the Telangana High Court granted an interim stay on implementing the schemes after the state had not filed its counter-affidavit [1]."
  - "The state later filed its counter; the single judge did not immediately vacate the stay, and the state appealed to a division bench. The registry initially declined to number the appeal, and Chief Justice Aparesh Kumar Singh and Justice G. M. Mohiuddin ordered it to be numbered [1]."
  - "In the state's argument Advocate General A. Sudarshan Reddy said the petition was not maintainable before a single judge, that the schemes have operated since 2014, and questioned the petitioner's standing [1][2]."
  - "In a report dated 2 September 2026, the division bench stayed the single judge's interim order and stayed further proceedings in the writ petition before the single judge [2]."
  - "The petitioner said no specific Comptroller and Auditor General report declares the schemes unconstitutional, but contended that the CAG flagged about Rs 94 crore as unaccounted expenditure linked to the schemes; this is his submission as reported, not a finding [2]."
unknowns:

  - "Whether the High Court will rule on the schemes' legal basis, and whether the state will seek to place them on a statutory footing."
  - "The CAG's actual findings on the schemes, which were not located and are not confirmed here."

The schemes were begun under the state government that held office in 2014 and have continued under later state governments; the case concerns the legal form of the Government Orders, not the schemes' merits. This item records status as of 12 October 2026. No court order text was available to the author; reports are from a single news outlet and should be checked against the High Court's orders.
