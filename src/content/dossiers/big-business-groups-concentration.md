## In depth

Indian business is often discussed in terms of a few large groups. Measured by market capitalisation at the end of 2025, Reliance, Bharti, Adani and the Tata group were each reported in the Rs 14 lakh crore to Rs 28 lakh crore range, though the sources use different dates and bases [4]. In the Nifty 50 list of August 2026, HDFC and ICICI banking companies carried the largest single-group weights (10.5% and 9.1%), with Reliance 8.8% and Tata 7.5% [2].

Shares within individual sectors differ widely: TRAI data show the two largest mobile operators holding roughly three-quarters of wireless subscribers [3], while a company-reported 27% to 28% of port cargo and 24% of airport passengers are attributed to Adani entities. One analysis finds the five largest groups' share of non-financial assets rose from about 10% in 1991 to nearly 18% in 2021, while the next five fell [1].

The 1950s and 1960s are the reference point for older measures: Hazari's study treated the family-controlled group as the unit of analysis, the Monopolies Inquiry Commission reported in 1965 and the MRTP Act followed in 1969 [6][11]. Its expansion controls were made inoperative after July 1991 and the Act was replaced by the Competition Act in 2009 [11]. Figures from those decades are not directly comparable with today's market-capitalisation measures.

## Do these groups run India?

Facts cited for the proposition that large groups hold a dominant position: rising Big-5 asset share since 1991, Top-5 sales shares of over 84% in telecom and over 65% in retail trade [1], and leading shares in ports, airports and refining. Facts against: the largest listed group weights are 10.5% or less of the Nifty 50 [2]; group revenues are 3% to 5% of nominal GDP [10]; Big 6-10 shares fell [1]; and no official total for group employment, tax or capex shares was obtained, so those are not stated. Named economists, parties and the government take differing positions, listed under positions. The numbers here do not establish who influences decisions.
