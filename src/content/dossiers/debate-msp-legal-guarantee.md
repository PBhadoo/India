## In depth

Minimum Support Price (MSP) is the price at which the Union government announces it will buy specified crops. It is announced for 22 mandated crops on the recommendation of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices and approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs [1][3]. Announcement is an executive decision: it does not oblige any buyer, public or private, to purchase at that price, and actual purchase by the Food Corporation and state agencies has been concentrated in some crops and states [3][5].

The debate is about whether MSP should become a legal entitlement, and the options put forward are different in kind. One is to keep the present system, in which the Government fixes MSP at not less than 1.5 times the all-India weighted average cost of production, as announced in the Union Budget 2018-19 [1][2]. A second is a statutory guarantee, which the Standing Committee on Agriculture (2024) and farmer organisations support in some form, and which the Doubling Farmers' Income Committee warned could raise retail inflation [3]. A third family of options narrows the obligation: a payment of the gap between MSP and market price, an obligation on private buyers, or widening state procurement to more crops [2][6].

Both the INC-led governments (2004-2014) and BJP-led governments (1998-2004; 2014 onward) have been cited on the question. The Swaminathan Commission, set up under the UPA, recommended in 2006 a return of at least 50% over cost [3]; the minister of the current BJP-led government said in 2024 that the UPA declined this, while his government adopted a 1.5-times principle in 2018 [2][4]. The INC, in opposition, promised a legal guarantee in its 2024 manifesto [10]. The sources opened do not show what the Rao and Vajpayee governments decided on this specific question.

The numbers that bear on the question include the share of farmers who sell at MSP (6% in a 2015 committee report), the volumes procured (for example 262 lakh tonnes of wheat in 2024-25), and costings that depend on assumptions [5][8][6].
