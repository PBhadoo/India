Allegations from parties across eras are listed with responses but none is established by a finding unless stated."

The Anoop Baranwal judgment records the Commission's own request for an independent secretariat and for its expenditure to be charged on the Consolidated Fund, but the sources reviewed do not show whether that has been adopted."

## Explainer

Article 324 of the Constitution vests the superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections to Parliament, the state legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission. The Commission has had three members since 1993, a structure the Supreme Court upheld in 1995 [23]. Appointments were made by the President, without a parliamentary law, until the Supreme Court's 2 March 2023 judgment in Anoop Baranwal set an interim committee including the Chief Justice [3]. Parliament then passed the 2023 Act, which put a Union Cabinet Minister in the Chief Justice's place, and its validity is pending before the Supreme Court, which refused a stay in March 2024 [1][4][5]. The Chief Election Commissioner can be removed only as a Supreme Court judge can; other Commissioners only on the CEC's recommendation [1]. Removal notices against the present CEC were submitted in 2026 and rejected by the presiding officers on 6 April, with a fresh notice filed on 24 April [12][13].

Disputes have arisen in every period covered here. Complaints about the Commission's partiality were made by the BJP in 2006 to 2009 against a Commissioner appointed under a Congress-led government, by the BJP in 2014, by the Congress in 2019 and by opposition parties from 2023 to 2026 [22][24][20][12]. In each case the Commission or the government named in the complaint replied, and the courts have given findings only on narrower questions, such as the EVM-VVPAT system in April 2024 [6]. The Commission's 2024 position on Form 17C data is described in [8]. Electoral bonds data were published by the Commission after the Supreme Court's February 2024 order; see the Electoral Bond Scheme, 2018 action entry for detail [27]. The special intensive revision of rolls is covered in the dossier special-intensive-revision-electoral-rolls and is not repeated here.
