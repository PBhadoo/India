## In depth

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) is a central agency under the Ministry of Finance that enforces the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act [3]. The PMLA was enacted in January 2003 under the Vajpayee-era government and brought into force on 1 July 2005 under the Manmohan Singh-era government [4]. It has been amended several times, including through the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 [7].

The Supreme Court's 27 July 2022 judgment in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary upheld the ED's powers of arrest, attachment and the stringent bail conditions, held ED officers are not police officers, and said the ECIR need not be supplied to the accused [1]. Review petitions were still being heard on preliminary issues in 2025 [14]. Later benches have set limits: written grounds of arrest are required [2], bail may be read into s.45 after long delay [16], and a bench said the ED was crossing limits in a state-agency probe [17].

Official data show a rise in registered PMLA cases and searches after 2014, with a larger count of convictions in the later period than the single digits in earlier ones; however, the counts use different measures and periods [5][6][7][8]. The government and the ED say verdicts in completed trials support their cases and that the agency does not act on political affiliation [10][11]. Opposition parties and some analysts say the proportion of opposition politicians among those investigated has risen [13][20]. The government has told Parliament it keeps no party-wise data [9][10].

State agencies have also arrested opposition leaders, in states governed by different parties [21]. This entry reports the figures and statements side by side and does not draw a conclusion on motive.
