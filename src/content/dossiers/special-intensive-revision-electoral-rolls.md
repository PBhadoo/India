claims:
  - claim: "The SIR is constitutionally and statutorily valid; the ECI may examine citizenship only to decide roll eligibility and must refer doubtful-citizenship cases to the competent authority under the Citizenship Act, 1955."
    type: finding
    madeBy: "Supreme Court of India, judgment of 27 May 2026"
    status: "Judgment of 27 May 2026 delivered and opened (2026 INSC 564; CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi). Para 186 holds the exercise traceable to s.21(3) read with Article 324 and proportionate, finds the document regime not arbitrary (with Aadhaar added by the order of 8 September 2025), and says the ECI's citizenship enquiry is limited to electoral eligibility. It directs the ECI to refer within four weeks to the competent authority under the Citizenship Act cases of names deleted from the 2003 roll as non-citizens, the authority to decide preferably before the next Parliamentary, Assembly or local-body election after notice and hearing, with inclusion if found a citizen; persons erroneously deleted as absent, dead, shifted or duplicate may seek judicial review. Secondary reports word some details differently."
    source: [18, 28]
  - claim: "The SIR is needed because of rapid migration, urbanisation, duplicate entries, deaths not recorded and possible inclusion of foreign nationals, and ensures that all eligible citizens are on the roll and no ineligible person is."
    type: official-statement
    madeBy: "Election Commission of India (order of 24 June 2025 as reported); ECI stated objective that 'the names of all eligible citizens are included'"
    status: "Objective confirmed in ECI statement reported by ETV Bharat (opened again: it cites the order of 24 June but not its legal provisions). ## In depth

On 24 June 2025 the Election Commission of India (ECI) ordered a Special Intensive Revision of the Bihar electoral roll, about four months before the state's Assembly election, in the term of the NDA government (centre-2024-modi-3). The ECI is a constitutional body under Article 324; its power over rolls derives from Article 324 and, as the Supreme Court later held, section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950. The ECI order text was not opened. The 27 May 2026 judgment (opened) distinguishes the SIR's 'special revision' under section 21(3) from ordinary revision under section 21(2) read with rule 25 of the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960; whether the ECI's order itself cites rule 25 is not confirmed here.

The SIR differs from the usual annual summary revision. Electors received pre-printed enumeration forms from Booth Level Officers (BLOs), and the roll was rebuilt from those forms. Electors traceable to the previous intensive roll (2003 in Bihar) did not need to submit documents; others were asked for one of eleven listed documents, with a different set of requirements depending on date of birth. The Supreme Court directed on 8 September 2025 that Aadhaar be accepted as proof of identity but not of citizenship (the Observer report describes it as a twelfth document).

Figures appear in the tables above. In Bihar the roll fell from 7.89 crore to about 7.42 crore; Phase 2 and 3 rolls show larger percentage reductions in several States, attributed by the ECI mainly to absent, shifted, dead and duplicate entries.

Petitioners and opposition parties allege exclusion of eligible voters, short timelines and BLO overwork. The ECI says the exercise protects the purity of the roll and that claims and appeals remain open. On 27 May 2026 the Supreme Court upheld the SIR with limits on the ECI's citizenship inquiry. Proceedings on West Bengal appeals continue, with a hearing listed on 16 October 2026.

Status by phase on 12 October 2026: Bihar complete (final roll 30 September 2025); Phase 2 final rolls published in the listed States and UTs, with West Bengal appeals still before tribunals; Phase 3 under way, with final-roll dates revised in several States (Karnataka and Delhi 27 October; Maharashtra 4 November, reported moved to 16 November; see the schedule table, not verified against ECI orders).
